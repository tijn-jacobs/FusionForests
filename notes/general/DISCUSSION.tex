\section{Discussion}

We develop a Bayesian nonparametric framework that combines randomised and observational survival data to estimate heterogeneous treatment effects on survival outcomes.
We relax the common assumption of unconfoundedness in the observational source.
The confounding function absorbs the observational bias.
A hierarchical prior borrows the prognostic baseline across sources while allowing it to differ between them.
The Bayesian fusion forest improves CATE estimation accuracy over a trial-only analysis in simulation studies.
It is robust to the strength of confounding and to between-source heterogeneity.
Posterior inference delivers finite-sample uncertainty quantification at the patient and population levels.
We illustrate the framework on the ACTG~175 trial augmented with the MACS cohort.
The interval-censored cohort extends follow-up well beyond the trial.

Our framework implicitly assumes the acceleration factor $\exp\{\tau(X)\}$ is time-invariant: treatment rescales the survival time by a factor that depends on the covariates but not on time.
This assumption may be inappropriate when treatment effects unfold over time, such as delayed-onset benefits or waning effects.
Flexible parametric AFT models relax this assumption by letting the acceleration factor vary with time \citep{pang2021flexible, Crowther2023, akynkozhayev2025flexible}.
The causal interpretation of time-dependent acceleration factors has been formalised \citep{Brathovde2024}.
A natural extension replaces $\tau(X)$ with a generalised Bayesian tree ensemble $\tau(X, t)$ \citep{linero2025generalized}.
